01 · Experiment

Direct command to /diff_drive_controller/cmd_vel, with NO Nav2, no mission and no velocity smoother. The robot stays on Gazebo's floor with wheel and caster collisions enabled.

02 · Closed-loop motor model

A first-order command filter emulates measured closed-loop RPM dynamics; it does not simulate motor torque or make acceleration depend on tire load. Compare its joint feedback to MATLAB before accepting the fit. Typical 2% settling: 0.2 s ≈ τ 0.05 s.

03 · Floor and drivetrain physics

Contact friction acts at tire/caster-floor contacts. Joint damping is bearing-like viscous resistance—not ground friction. Contact slip adjusts force-dependent rolling/lateral slip (not a viscous damping coefficient). Wheel width affects contact geometry. Gazebo's default velocity interface can largely mask damping in wheel RPM; measure the separate ground-truth robot motion to assess contact changes.

Physics changes require respawning the floor-loaded robot.

04 · Publish to main simulation

After you validate the wheel response and floor contact, explicitly publish the saved calibration. This updates only src/scrobot_simulation/config/mobility_calibration.yaml, with a backup of the previous values.

Publishing does not change the real robot, main court scene, or live Gazebo process. Rebuild as needed and restart the normal simulation to apply. If the first-order motor model is selected, the main simulation inserts a simulation-only motor response between the collision monitor and differential-drive controller.
